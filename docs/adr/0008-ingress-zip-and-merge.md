# ADR 0008: Ingress ZIP profile, ingestion scopes and fixed-part merge

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-01
- Task / spike: M1.4

## Context

02 §5 fixes the archive profile and budgets, and 02 §6 fixes the merge procedure. Building them
left choices that the design leaves open:

- **Data descriptors.** 02 §5 doesn't say whether they are allowed. All 16 archives that
  `actions/upload-artifact` v4 produced for the prototype runs (PS 36405830015; TP 36287837535,
  36301239732, 36314265418; `.reference/`, ADR 0001) use them on every entry:
  - general-purpose bit 3, with zero CRC and sizes in the local header;
  - a signed 16-byte descriptor after the data;
  - method 8, version needed 20, made by 0x032D (Unix), external attributes 0x81A40020
    (regular file 0644 plus the DOS archive bit);
  - no extra fields or comments;
  - contiguous entries in central-directory order, with the EOCD as the last 22 bytes.

  A reader that refused descriptors would refuse every real artifact.
- **Which ZIP reader.** 02 §5 says the reader is part of the TCB: "vendor or pin it, and test it".
- **Which failures reject one part and which refuse the whole ingestion.**
- **Conflicts.** How to resolve two parts that list one unit key.
- **Units absent on both sides.** 02 §4 says these aren't units, but doesn't say what the
  merge does with them.
- **Where images are decoded and stored.**
- **Parts limits disagree.** config@1 allowed 32 providers × 64 shards (up to 4096 parts per
  revision), while run@1 and snapshot@1 record at most 256 `parts` and `missingParts`.

## Decision

### ZIP reader

**Own reader on `node:zlib`** (`packages/core/src/ingest/zip.ts`), with no new dependency. It is
not run in a worker.

- It works in two passes, like the PNG decoder:
  - Pass 1 validates structure and layout. It then inflates every entry in 64 KiB pieces,
    discarding the output, and counts the actual bytes against per-entry and per-ingestion
    budgets and CRC-32.
  - Pass 2 extracts one entry on demand into an exact-size buffer and re-checks its size and CRC.
  - Nothing ever holds an expanded archive.
- Parsing is linear, and inflation is asynchronous and cancellable between chunks. A worker
  would mean copying archives of up to 128 MiB. Decoding and encoding PNGs stay in the
  `PngWorker`.
- The profile is upload-artifact's layout, required strictly:
  - single disk, no comments, no ZIP64 structures or sentinels;
  - methods 0 and 8 only, version needed ≤ 20;
  - flags limited to bits 1–3 and 11; no encryption (bits 0, 6, 13, method 99);
  - host DOS or Unix, regular files only;
  - no extra fields (this blocks 0x7075 name smuggling);
  - each local header repeats its central record;
  - entries tile the bytes from 0 to the central directory with no gap, overlap, leading or
    trailing bytes.
- **Data descriptors** are allowed because real artifacts need them. They are allowed safely:
  - data is located by the central directory's compressed size, and never by scanning for a
    descriptor signature;
  - with bit 3, local CRC and sizes must be zero or equal to central;
  - the signed 16-byte descriptor must follow the data and equal the central record;
  - unsigned and ZIP64-form descriptors are refused.
- **Names** must be exactly `bundle.json` or `u-<64 hex>.png`. Duplicates and ASCII case-fold
  collisions are checked before the allowlist and before any extraction. Error messages carry
  entry indices, never names.
- Checks run in a fixed order and each failure has its own code. The committed hostile corpus
  (`testdata/zip/hostile/`, generated by `tools/zip-corpus/generate.ts`) pins each code.

### Scopes

`IngressError.scope` sets what a failure affects.

- **Part scope:** a malformed archive, invalid `bundle.json`, identity mismatch, file-set
  mismatch, an image outside the PNG profile, or a conflict. The part is rejected with a bounded
  diagnostic, and valid siblings form an explicitly incomplete run (02 §5).
- **Ingestion scope:** the whole ingestion is refused, and nothing is published (02 §5 "Work").
  This covers:
  - more than 4096 entries, or more than 512 MiB actually inflated, summed across every archive
    opened (a rejected part's bytes still count, so the outcome doesn't depend on order);
  - more than 256 MiB of selected archives, more than 1024 listed artifacts, or more than 2000
    declared units;
  - cancellation or the 10-minute deadline;
  - a PNG worker crash, timeout or abort (these say nothing about the part).

### Selection and merge

**Selection** uses names only. Other attempts, unexpected parts, other bundle versions and
non-PixelWatch artifacts are ignored with a reason and **never opened**. Two artifacts with one
part's name reject that part, and neither is picked.

**Conflicts.** When two valid parts of one revision list the same unit key, **both** are
rejected (`part-conflict`). The result is the same whatever the order or artifact IDs.

**Missing sides.**

- A side no valid part lists is `missing/part-missing` when an expected part of that revision and
  provider isn't valid, because the unit may have been in it.
- Otherwise it is `missing/unit-missing`. This is the reading of 02 §6 step 4: "a listed unit
  without a result is `missing`".
- A missing part's catalog is unknown. It adds no units and reports no count.

**Both-absent units** (owner decision, 2026-10-01). A unit absent on both sides, or absent with
no baseline, is left out of the declared set and counted in `Ingestion.excluded` (count plus up
to 16 keys). It is not a unit (02 §4), and `sideResult` refuses it.

### Images and blobs

**Images are admitted in M1.4.** Every captured PNG goes through the bounded decoder, the
`PngWorker` in production. It is hashed and stored as a canonical blob (`storeBlob`: reuse,
never overwrite). The side records the pixel hash and the **decoded** dimensions. Later stages
(M1.5 comparisons, projection) read canonical blobs only and never see uploaded bytes.

If a part is rejected after some of its images were stored, those blobs are unreferenced. Store
GC sweeps them (03 §5). That is harmless, because blobs are content-addressed and never
overwritten.

### Parts cap

The owner chose (2026-10-01) to add a config@1 semantic check: the shards across providers may
total at most 128 (`too-many-parts`, `MAX_CONFIG_SHARDS`), so base and head parts fit run@1's
256. 02 §1 says so.

## Consequences

- Code:
  - `packages/core/src/ingest/` holds `zip.ts`, `errors.ts`, `limits.ts`, `select.ts`,
    `part.ts`, `merge.ts`, `ingest.ts` and `types.ts`;
  - `ingestArtifacts(input)` is the one place untrusted archives become trusted side states;
  - `mergeParts` is pure and property-tested;
  - the forge (M2.1) supplies `{artifactName, artifactId, zip}` from the API listing, and the
    publisher passes a `PngWorker` and the store's blob pool.
- M1.5 builds snapshots and runs from `Ingestion`:
  - each unit's result is `unitResult(unit, unit.base, unit.head, comparison?)`;
  - `PartReport` maps to `run.parts` and `snapshot.parts`;
  - `coverage` is already in run@1's shape.
- Evidence:
  - `docs/evidence/m1.4-ingest-bench.md` records peak RSS and time for the largest allowed
    ingestion and the bombs;
  - `docs/evidence/m1.4-reference-ingest.md` records the real-data run.
- Design docs:
  - 02 §1 states the 128-shard cap;
  - 02 §5 and §6 point here for the descriptor allowance, scopes, conflicts and both-absent
    units.
- If GitHub's writer changes (for example, adds extra fields or ZIP64), real artifacts will be
  refused with a specific code. Accepting the new form needs a new ADR with evidence.
